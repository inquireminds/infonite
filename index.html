SDGs

Learning with World Goals[cite: 1]

Units link directly to the UN's 17 Sustainable Development Goals (e.g., clean water, climate action) so students see why learning matters[cite: 1].

BTC

Building Thinking Classrooms[cite: 1]

A research-based way to teach math (Dr. Peter Liljedahl)[cite: 1]. Students stand and solve problems in small, often-changing groups at whiteboards[cite: 1].

At Home: "We stood and solved a problem" means real thinking happened[cite: 1]!
21C

21st Century Transferable Skills[cite: 1]

Critical thinking, creativity, communication, self-directed learning, and digital skills—the transferable skills Ontario asks us to build[cite: 1].

At Home: Ask "How did you figure that out?"[cite: 1]
TEAM

Active Collaboration[cite: 1]

Students explain their thinking, listen, and build on each other's ideas[cite: 1]. Everyone has a role, while each student is assessed on their own growth[cite: 1].

Assessment: Collaborative learning builds depth; individual growth is evaluated separately[cite: 1].
UDL

Universal Design for Learning[cite: 1]

Lessons are planned so more students succeed from the start[cite: 1]. Choices in how to learn and show learning (write, speak, draw, build, video)[cite: 1]. High expectations for everyone[cite: 1].

Science & Technology Long Range Plan

Starting Focus: Heat in the Environment & Water Systems[cite: 2]

Grade 7

Starting Unit: How Heat Moves and Affects Our Environment (Strand E)[cite: 2]

Particle Theory of Matter:

Exploring how kinetic energy and thermal energy alter particle spacing, expansion, contraction, and changes of state.

Heat Transfer Mechanisms:

Investigating conduction, convection, and radiation in natural ecosystems and modern engineering.

Energy Efficiency & Insulation (SDG 7):

Evaluating thermal insulation, passive solar heating, and reducing heat loss in local housing[cite: 3].

Climate Action & Greenhouse Effect (SDG 13):

Assessing human impacts on global temperatures and sustainable energy alternatives[cite: 3].

Additional Grade 7 Units: Living things & environment interdependence | Pure substances and mixtures | Form and function of structures[cite: 2].

Grade 8

Starting Unit: Water Systems – How People Use and Protect Water (Strand E)[cite: 2]

Global & Local Watersheds (SDG 6):

Mapping glacial reserves, groundwater, water tables, and local watershed dynamics[cite: 3].

Water Quality & Testing:

Testing turbidity, pH, dissolved oxygen, bio-indicators, and industrial wastewater treatment.

Fluids & Global Climate Dynamics:

Examining heat capacity of water and ocean currents in moderating regional weather patterns.

Sustainability & Conservation:

Analyzing community water consumption and safeguarding local bodies like Lake Ontario.

Additional Grade 8 Units: Cells: the building blocks of life | Fluids | Systems in action[cite: 2].

TRY IT AT HOME - SDG 6[cite: 3]

"A school rain barrel holds 200 L[cite: 3]. Its roof is 20 m²[cite: 3]. Each 1 mm of rain on 1 m² gives 1 L[cite: 3]. If 5 mm of rain falls, how much water is collected? How many rainy days like that fill the barrel?"[cite: 3]

Answer: 20 × 5 = 100 L per day, so 2 days (if nothing overflows)[cite: 3].

How You Can Help[cite: 4]

Supporting your child's learning journey at home[cite: 4].

5 Ways You Can Support[cite: 4]

  1. Ask about thinking: Try "What was tricky today?" or "What did your group try first?"[cite: 4]
  2. Let them struggle a little: Being stuck is part of learning. A hint is better than the answer[cite: 4].
  3. Use your home language: Talking about science and math in your language builds understanding and does not slow English learning[cite: 4].
  4. Find it in daily life: Cooking, shopping, weather, transit times, and savings all count[cite: 4].
  5. Celebrate mistakes: Mistakes show what to learn next. Sleep, breakfast, and reading help too[cite: 4].

Great Questions for the Dinner Table[cite: 4]

  • • What problem did you solve today?[cite: 4]
  • • What did you try that did not work, and what did you do next?[cite: 4]
  • • Who did you learn something from today?[cite: 4]
  • • Where could we notice this at home?[cite: 4]
